## Mission

The American Physical Society is the disciplinary home for physics in the United States and a major international physics society. APS publishes the Physical Review family — the most cited physics journal series globally — including Physical Review Letters (PRL), the Physical Review series (A/B/C/D/E for subfields), and Physical Review Research (PRR, the OA generalist launched 2019).

APS's editorial culture emphasizes technical correctness over broad significance. Compared to Nature Physics's broad-significance-or-reject stance, APS journals (especially PRR) are more accepting of subfield-significant work, making them an important venue for the bulk of physics research.

## Society-wide Policies

### AI Use Policy

Disclosure-based: AI-assisted writing requires acknowledgment; AI as author prohibited.

**Source URL**: https://journals.aps.org/authors/editorial-policies-practices#ai-use

### Authorship Policy

APS standard ICMJE-aligned authorship; corresponding author manages submission; group-author policy for collaborations.

### Open Access Posture

PRR is full Gold OA (mandatory APC). PRL and specialty PR journals are hybrid (subscription default with optional Open Access via APC). APS membership provides APC discounts. APS supports green OA (preprint posting permitted and encouraged via arXiv).

### Conflict of Interest / Publication Ethics

APS Code of Ethics governs authorship, data integrity, publication ethics. COPE member.

## Editorial Culture

### Common Reviewer Expectations Across Society Venues

APS reviewers (Divisional Associate Editors + community reviewers) focus on technical correctness, methodological rigor, appropriate citation of prior work. PRL reviewers additionally evaluate broad-physics significance and rapid-communication-format fit. PRR reviewers focus primarily on technical correctness without significance-threshold.

### Acceptance Rate Patterns

PRL: ~25% (after editorial triage; ~50% desk-rejected). PRR: ~50-55% (more accepting; focus on soundness).

### Inter-venue Cascade / Transfer

PRL editors can recommend transfer to PRR (or specialty PR journals) upon rejection. Authors can opt into transfer at submission.
