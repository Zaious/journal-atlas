### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes (soft)
- **If yes, what kind?** Manuscripts must position a clear **HCI contribution** — empirical (system evaluation, user study), artifact (design, technique), methodological, or theoretical. Papers without explicit HCI stake (e.g., pure cognitive psychology with computer-screen stimuli) are misaligned.
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: Desk reject if HCI contribution is unclear; reviewers will probe "What is the HCI contribution?" if framing is incidental.

### Reviewer Pool Characteristics

Reviewers are drawn primarily from the ACM SIGCHI community — international HCI faculty and researchers active in CHI / CSCW / UIST / IUI / DIS conferences. Expect broad methodological literacy (controlled experiments, mixed methods, design research) and deep HCI canon knowledge. Common reviewer demands: "What is the HCI contribution?", "How does this generalize?", "Where is the user study?". Third-wave / critical HCI reviewers increasingly visible but mainstream empirical traditions remain the editorial center of gravity.

## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Manuscripts without clear HCI contribution claim
- Papers framed primarily for non-HCI audiences (cognitive psychology, design studies, etc.)
- Failure to disclose AI use per ACM policy
- Submissions requiring extensive non-HCI methodological development without HCI grounding

### Soft Tax

Variable. For HCI-canonical empirical papers: low. For third-wave / critical / autoethnographic HCI: moderate — requires explicit engagement with HCI canon and clear contribution articulation. For non-HCI-originating manuscripts: high — needs substantial reframing through HCI lens (design implications, user study, interaction technique). ACM article template adherence is a small but real production cost.

### Best Suited For

- Empirical HCI papers (controlled experiments, mixed-methods field studies, longitudinal deployments)
- Design research and artifact-evaluation papers
- HCI theory / methodology contributions
- CSCW, accessibility, value-sensitive design work
- Comprehensive HCI reviews and meta-analyses
- AI / ML interaction, mixed-reality, mobile and ubiquitous computing studies

### Not Recommended For

- Pure cognitive-psychology or social-psychology papers without HCI artifact or interaction
- Pure design studies without empirical evaluation
- Manuscripts targeting non-HCI audiences primarily
- Pure autoethnography without HCI canonical engagement
- Authors needing rapid turnaround (TOCHI revision cycle can extend across multiple rounds)

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [Human-Computer Interaction (T&F)](./human-computer-interaction.md) | Comparable top-tier HCI scope; T&F publisher (vs ACM); accommodates similar empirical and theoretical work |
| 2 | [International Journal of Human-Computer Studies](./international-journal-of-human-computer-studies.md) | Elsevier IJHCS; cognitive-leaning HCI; welcomes formal-model and theoretical work |
| 3 | [International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction](./international-journal-of-human-computer-interaction.md) | T&F IJHCI; broader design-leaning HCI scope; somewhat more accommodating |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a top HCI conference proceedings (CHI, CSCW, UIST) as "too long / too journal-format," TOCHI is often a better fit because: its rolling submission model accommodates extended analyses; comprehensive reviews and longer empirical papers are welcomed; and the same SIGCHI community reviews both venues, so reviewer familiarity transfers.
