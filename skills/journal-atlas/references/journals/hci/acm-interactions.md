## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Manuscripts in academic-paper register (too long, too technical, not opinionated)
- Pieces without clear "take" or contribution to HCI community discourse
- Visual-design-incompatible submissions (no figures, no narrative structure)
- Failure to disclose AI use per ACM policy

### Soft Tax

Low for thought pieces and reflective essays. Moderate for academic researchers — typically requires substantial register shift (jargon stripping, first-person voice, narrative hook, accessible language). Magazine format requires figures, photographs, or illustrations.

### Best Suited For

- HCI manifestos, provocations, critical commentary
- Practitioner reflections and "Day in the Lab" features
- Accessible field overviews and trend pieces
- Design-research provocations
- Cross-disciplinary HCI thought leadership

### Not Recommended For

- Full empirical research papers (consider TOCHI, HCI journal, IJHCS)
- Methodologically-rigorous user studies (wrong format)
- Manuscripts requiring extensive reference engagement
- Authors needing JCR-indexed impact-factor venues
- Submissions requiring formal academic peer review for tenure cases

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [Frontiers in Computer Science](./frontiers-in-computer-science.md) | If the piece is reframed as Hypothesis & Theory article; OA practitioner reach |
| 2 | [AI & Society](./ai-and-society.md) | If the piece engages AI / society / ethics; Springer; broader-than-HCI audience |
| 3 | [ACM TOCHI](./acm-transactions-on-computer-human-interaction.md) | If the piece can be developed into a full empirical or theoretical research paper |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your full research paper was rejected from TOCHI or HCI journal as "more provocation than evaluation," ACM Interactions is often a better fit because: it explicitly welcomes opinionated thought pieces; its accessible register avoids the empirical-rigor demands; and the SIGCHI editorial team values community-resonant ideas over methodological completeness.
