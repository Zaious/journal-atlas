### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes (soft)

## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Manuscripts without IT/behavior framing
- Pure design or pure cognitive-engineering papers
- Failure to disclose AI use per T&F publisher policy

### Soft Tax

Low for technology-adoption / social-media empirical authors. Moderate for HCI-canonical work needing behavioral framing.

### Best Suited For

- Technology adoption empirical studies
- Social-media behavior and digital-marketing research
- Adolescent technology impact studies
- Digital wellbeing and screen-time work
- Knowledge management with IT focus
- Mid-tier-publication outlet for solid empirical IT/behavior work

### Not Recommended For

- Pure HCI design provocations
- Pure cognitive engineering
- Theoretical-philosophical pieces
- Authors needing top-tier HCI reputation signaling (consider TOCHI, HCI journal)

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction](./international-journal-of-human-computer-interaction.md) | Same T&F family; closer HCI scope |
| 2 | [Computers in Human Behavior](./computers-in-human-behavior.md) | Larger Elsevier outlet; technology-psychology crossover |
| 3 | [International Journal of Human-Computer Studies](./international-journal-of-human-computer-studies.md) | If the work has cognitive-engineering angle |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a top HCI journal as "more behavioral than HCI," BIT is often a better fit because: its scope explicitly bridges behavior and IT; technology adoption is a core topic; and the editorial culture rewards applied empirical contributions.
