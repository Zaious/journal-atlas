### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes (soft)
- **If yes, what kind?** Manuscripts must demonstrate **applied HCI contribution** — technology adoption, automation safety, VR/AR user studies, or AI-service interaction. Less theoretical-canonical than HCI journal (T&F); more empirical-applied than TOCHI.
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: Manuscripts outside applied-HCI scope may be redirected; pure design or pure theory pieces less welcome.

### AI-Research Notes (supplementary AI research, 2026-07-13)

> Added as a **supplementary, independent research pass** — it does NOT overwrite or supersede the Tier assessment above. Treat conflicts as a signal to re-verify, not as an automatic correction. Overall `signal_quality` for this pass: **3/5**.

**AI-research finding (policy):** Publisher-level (Taylor & Francis) policy, not confirmed journal-specific (hihc20 author-instructions page returned 403 and could not be verified directly). Generative AI may not be listed as author (no accountability/copyright capacity). Permitted uses: idea generation/exploration, language/grammar improvement, LLM-enhanced literature search, literature classification, coding/script-writing support. Prohibited: generating or manipulating research data, images, figures, visuals. Mandatory disclosure of tool name/version, purpose and method, placed in Methods or Acknowledgments. T&F explicitly notes individual journals may be stricter (language-improvement-only) so this is a publisher default, not a verified hihc20-specific rule.

**AI-research finding (positioning — what the journal accepts now):** Journal is actively and heavily publishing AI/LLM-related HCI work as of 2025-2026: e.g. 'Human-Generative AI Interaction: Assessing Futureproofing Attributes from Time Perspectives and Temporal Distances' (Vol 42 No 10, 2025); studies on chatbot response latency and customer evaluation; LLM-driven conversational agents for depression/mental-health support; user trust in LLM-based conversational agents. Core scope remains interactive computing (cognitive/ergonomic), digital accessibility, UX design, haptics, and sociology of the internet/technology.

## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Manuscripts outside applied-HCI / IS / VR/AR / AI-service scope
- Pure design provocations without empirical evaluation
- Failure to disclose AI use per T&F publisher policy
- Pure theoretical critique without applied implications

### Soft Tax

Low for technology-adoption empirical authors. Moderate for HCI-canonical empirical work that needs adoption / automation / VR / AI-services framing. T&F editorial cycle moderate.

### Best Suited For

- Technology adoption studies (TAM, UTAUT extensions)
- Human-automation interaction and safety
- VR / AR / immersive UX evaluation
- AI-service interaction studies
- Cross-cultural technology adoption work
- Quantitative usability studies with strong empirical methodology

### Not Recommended For

- Pure design-research provocations
- Critical / decolonial HCI theory pieces
- Autoethnographic or first-person work
- Pure cognitive engineering without applied scope (consider IJHCS)
- Authors needing rapid OA visibility (T&F hybrid default)

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [Behaviour and Information Technology](./behaviour-and-information-technology.md) | Same T&F family; broader IT/HCI scope; mid-tier accommodation |
| 2 | [International Journal of Human-Computer Studies](./international-journal-of-human-computer-studies.md) | Closer to cognitive-engineering side; Elsevier; complementary scope |
| 3 | [Computers in Human Behavior](./computers-in-human-behavior.md) | Larger Elsevier outlet; technology-psychology crossover; broader scope |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a top Information Systems journal as "too HCI-focused," IJHCI is often a better fit because: its scope explicitly bridges HCI and IS; technology adoption and human-automation are core topics; and its editorial culture rewards applied empirical contributions over pure-IS theoretical work.
