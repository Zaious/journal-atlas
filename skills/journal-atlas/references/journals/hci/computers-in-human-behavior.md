### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes
- **If yes, what kind?** Manuscripts must demonstrate **psychological behavioral phenomena related to technology use**. Pure HCI design or pure cognitive engineering misaligned; pure psychology without technology focus also misaligned.

## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Manuscripts without behavioral-psychology framing
- Pure design or pure cognitive engineering
- Qualitative-only single-case studies
- AI-generated images per Elsevier policy
- Failure to disclose AI use

### Soft Tax

Low for quantitative-psychology authors. Very high for qualitative or design-research authors — typically requires complete restructuring with quantitative scaffolding.

### Best Suited For

- Adolescent and child technology-use empirical studies
- Social-media psychology and digital-behavior research
- Mental health × technology empirical work
- Technology adoption (TAM/UTAUT) studies
- Meta-analyses of digital-behavior literature
- High-volume publication outlet for solid quantitative work

### Not Recommended For

- Qualitative or autoethnographic work
- HCI design provocations
- Cognitive engineering (consider IJHCS)
- Theoretical-philosophical pieces
- Authors needing low-volume / high-prestige reputation signaling

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [Behaviour and Information Technology](./behaviour-and-information-technology.md) | T&F counterpart; broader IT/behavior scope; mid-tier accommodation |
| 2 | [International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction](./international-journal-of-human-computer-interaction.md) | If the work has stronger HCI angle |
| 3 | [Frontiers in Psychology](../psychology/frontiers-in-psychology.md) | If the work is psychology-dominant with technology context |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a top behavioral-psychology venue as "too applied / too technology-focused," CHB is often a better fit because: its scope is explicitly technology-behavior; large volume accommodates broad topic range; and reviewer pool is comfortable with technology-as-context.
