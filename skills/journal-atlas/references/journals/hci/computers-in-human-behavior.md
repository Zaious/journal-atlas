### Framing Requirements

- Mandatory framing: yes, stated. "The journal addresses human interactions with computers, not computers per se. The computer is discussed only as a medium through which human behaviors are shaped and expressed. The primary message of most articles involves information about human behavior" (https://www.sciencedirect.com/journal/computers-in-human-behavior/about/aims-and-scope, Wayback snapshot 2024-02-28).
- Two scope branches are named: use of computers in psychology, psychiatry and related disciplines, and the psychological impact of computer use on individuals, groups and society (same page).

## Strategic Notes

> Every statement below follows from a fact recorded above and names it. Judgements about the journal's culture are not made because the evidence for them is absent.

### Hard Blockers

- Work that is not about human behaviour: the aims say "the computer is discussed only as a medium" and the primary message must concern human behavior (Framing Requirements).
- Author-identifying information in the anonymised manuscript: double-anonymized review with a separate title page (Peer Review table; Practical Concerns).
- Missing ethics-approval statement with date and reference number and informed-consent statement for human-subject work (Practical Concerns, IRB row).
- Undisclosed generative-AI use in writing, or AI-generated images outside Elsevier's permitted cases (AI Policy table).
- Missing highlights: they are mandatory (Format table).

### Soft Tax

- Highlights, a 250-word abstract and APA 7 formatting (Format, Voice & Style).
- Replication, scale-validation and negative-result papers are routed to the companion title, so they carry a scope risk here (Framing Requirements).
- Timeline uncertainty: the journal's own page gives 62 days to a decision after review and 153 days to acceptance, while SciRev (n=6) rows range from 1 day for an immediate rejection to 60.1 weeks total for one rejected manuscript (Review Cycle Time).
- Open access, if wanted, costs USD 4,840 excluding taxes (Open Access table).

### Best Suited For

- Behavioural studies of AI, chatbots, robots and algorithms: 13.2% of titles (Sensitive Topics) and 7 of the 12 most-cited recent titles (Epistemological & Political Leanings).
- Social-media, adolescent-technology, gaming and well-being studies: 13.0%, 9.3%, 8.7% and 8.9% of titles (Sensitive Topics).
- Quantitative designs with mediation or moderation analysis: 6.9% of titles (Methodological Preferences).
- Systematic and literature reviews on technology and behaviour: 2.6% of titles and two of the top 12 (Methodological Preferences).

### Not Recommended For

- Qualitative-only or ethnographic work: those terms appear in 0.2-0.7% of titles (Methodological Preferences); this is rarity, not a stated exclusion.
- Replication studies, scale validations and negative-result papers: the aims route them to Computers in Human Behavior Reports (Framing Requirements).
- Design or cognitive-engineering work with no behavioural question: the aims restrict scope to human behaviour (Framing Requirements).
- Authors needing a short, predictable review time: the only evidence is the journal's insight figures and SciRev anecdotes (Review Cycle Time).
