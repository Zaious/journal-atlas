## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Manuscripts without empirical or formal-model contribution
- Pure design provocations or critical-theory pieces
- AI-generated images per Elsevier policy
- Failure to disclose AI use

### Soft Tax

Low for cognitive-engineering empirical authors. Moderate for design-research or third-wave HCI submissions — requires recasting through cognitive-engineering or formal-evaluation lens. 24-month AAM embargo is a visibility cost; preprint strategy recommended.

### Best Suited For

- Cognitive-engineering HCI empirical studies
- Formal models of interaction, computational HCI
- Applied perception, multimodal interaction, haptics
- Methodological contributions to HCI experimental design
- Cross-cultural HCI usability studies with strong empirical rigor

### Not Recommended For

- Pure design-research provocations
- Autoethnography or first-person work
- Critical / decolonial HCI theory pieces
- Authors needing rapid OA visibility (24-month AAM embargo)
- Manuscripts without experimental or formal-evaluation methodology

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [Human-Computer Interaction (T&F)](./human-computer-interaction.md) | Top-tier peer; broader methodological tolerance; no AAM embargo gap |
| 2 | [ACM TOCHI](./acm-transactions-on-computer-human-interaction.md) | ACM SIGCHI alternative; broader scope including design-research |
| 3 | [International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction](./international-journal-of-human-computer-interaction.md) | Same T&F family of journals but more design-leaning |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a top cognitive-psychology venue as "too HCI / too applied," IJHCS is often a better fit because: it welcomes cognitive-engineering empirical work explicitly; formal models of human performance are a journal strength; and the cognitive-HCI bridge is the journal's distinctive identity.
