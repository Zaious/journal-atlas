### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes (strong)
- **If yes, what kind?** Manuscripts must demonstrate **applied human factors / ergonomics contribution** — automation safety, physical ergonomics, traffic/aviation safety, occupational health, or applied performance. Critical-theory or qualitative-only work misaligned.
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: Desk reject likely outside HFES scope.

## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Manuscripts outside ergonomics / human factors / safety scope
- Pure design or critical-theory pieces
- Qualitative-only single-case studies
- Failure to disclose AI use per Sage publisher policy

### Soft Tax

Low for HFES-tradition authors. Very high for HCI design or critical work — typically requires complete restructuring with applied-ergonomics framing.

### Best Suited For

- Human-automation interaction and safety studies
- Physical ergonomics and occupational biomechanics
- Traffic, aviation, and operational safety research
- Applied human factors empirical work
- Accessibility / aging ergonomics
- Meta-analyses of safety / ergonomics literature

### Not Recommended For

- HCI design provocations
- Critical / decolonial HCI work
- Qualitative or autoethnographic submissions
- Pure cognitive engineering (consider IJHCS)
- Pure social-media / digital-behavior work (consider CHB)

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [International Journal of Human-Computer Studies](./international-journal-of-human-computer-studies.md) | Closer to cognitive-engineering; broader HCI scope |
| 2 | [Behaviour and Information Technology](./behaviour-and-information-technology.md) | If the work has IT-behavior angle; mid-tier |
| 3 | [International Journal of Human-Computer Interaction](./international-journal-of-human-computer-interaction.md) | Applied HCI; technology-adoption neighbor |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a top applied-psychology venue as "too applied / too ergonomics-focused," Human Factors is often a better fit because: its scope is explicitly ergonomics × human factors; safety and automation are core topics; and HFES affiliation provides built-in audience.
