### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes
- **If yes, what kind?** Manuscripts must foreground **qualitative methodology** — methodological innovation, reflexive engagement, or epistemological argument is required. Empirical findings without methodological reflection are misaligned.
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: Desk reject likely if manuscript reads as primary empirical report without methodological contribution; reviewer pushback on insufficient reflexive depth.

### Reviewer Pool Characteristics

Reviewers are drawn primarily from the autoethnography, qualitative-methodology, and posthumanist-research communities — the SAGE qualitative family network. Expect deep literacy in Anderson, Adams, Ellis, Bochner, Denzin (autoethnography), Braun & Clarke (thematic analysis), Richardson (writing-as-inquiry), Spry (performance autoethnography). Common reviewer demands: "Where is the reflexive depth?", "What is the methodological contribution?", "How does this engage the autoethnographic tradition rigorously?" Reviewers tolerant of experimental forms; intolerant of "qualitative as light empirical" submissions.

## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Quantitative-only submissions
- Empirical findings without methodological reflection or reflexive engagement
- Manuscripts that treat qualitative methods as light-empirical rather than as an epistemological commitment
- AI listed as author (per Sage publisher policy)

### Soft Tax

Low for autoethnographic / reflexive manuscripts originating in the qualitative-methodology tradition. For manuscripts originating in psychology or other empirical disciplines: moderate — typically requires recasting psychology-theoretical framing into qualitative-methodology framing (e.g., emphasizing the autoethnographic contribution over the psychological-theoretical contribution). Word limit (~8–10K estimated) requires trimming from longer manuscripts.

### Best Suited For

- Autoethnographic, performance ethnographic, and evocative-narrative work
- Methodological innovation papers in qualitative inquiry
- Reflexive accounts of fieldwork, insider research, dual-positionality
- Decolonial, posthumanist, critical-race qualitative scholarship
- Arts-based and experimental qualitative writing forms

### Not Recommended For

- Quantitative or mixed-methods (quantitative-dominant) work
- Submissions framed primarily as psychological-theoretical contributions
- Manuscripts requiring extensive engagement with psychological-empirical literature (the journal's reviewer pool may not be deeply versed in self-state theory, Bromberg, dialogical self)
- Submissions on BDSM / kink topics where domain-specific literature engagement is heavy (zero precedent 2023–2025)
- Authors needing rapid OA visibility (Sage hybrid subscription is the default)

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [Qualitative Research in Psychology](./qualitative-research-in-psychology.md) | Closest methodological cousin; T&F-published; explicit qualitative-psychology framing if the work has psychology grounding |
| 2 | [Cultural Studies ↔ Critical Methodologies](./cultural-studies-critical-methodologies.md) | Strong fit for critical / decolonial / performance-oriented work; same SAGE qualitative family |
| 3 | [Theory & Psychology](../psychology/theory-and-psychology.md) | If the contribution has theoretical-psychology stakes; broader theoretical scope; smaller methodological-reflexivity emphasis |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a more mainstream empirical-qualitative outlet (e.g., *Qualitative Health Research*, *Field Methods*), Qualitative Inquiry is often a better fit because: it welcomes experimental and evocative forms rejected as "too literary" by mainstream qualitative outlets; it values reflexive depth over methodological standardization; and its scope explicitly includes performance, arts-based, and posthumanist inquiry.
