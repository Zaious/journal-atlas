### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes
- **If yes, what kind?** Manuscripts must engage **cultural studies AND critical methodology** — the journal's title literalizes this requirement. Pure cultural-studies analysis without methodological critique, or pure methodological work without cultural-studies grounding, is misaligned.
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: Desk reject likely if the cultural-studies / critical-methodology bridge is absent; reviewers will probe the journal-fit if either side is decorative.

### Reviewer Pool Characteristics

Reviewers are drawn primarily from the cultural studies, critical-methodologies, and decolonial-autoethnography communities. Expect deep literacy in Denzin, Lincoln, Spry, Madison, performative writing, decolonial theory (Smith, Mbembe, Quijano), and posthumanist scholarship. Common reviewer demands: "Where is the critical edge?", "How does this disrupt or interrogate dominant methodologies?", "Where is the reflexive-political engagement?". Reviewers tolerant of experimental forms; intolerant of conventional empirical reporting.

## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Quantitative submissions
- Submissions over 8,000 words inclusive of notes and references (hard cap)
- Manuscripts lacking either cultural-studies grounding or critical-methodology engagement
- AI listed as author (per Sage publisher policy)
- Conventional empirical reporting without critical / reflexive framing

### Soft Tax

Moderate-to-high. The 8,000-word hard limit **including notes and references** is the dominant constraint — references alone typically consume 1,500–2,000 words, leaving main text at ~6,000 words. Manuscripts originating outside cultural-studies framing require recasting through critical-methodology vocabulary. Reviewers expect explicit engagement with critical / decolonial / performative canon.

### Best Suited For

- Critical, decolonial, postcolonial autoethnographies
- Performative writing, poetic inquiry, arts-based research
- Collaborative and collective autoethnographies
- Critical-methodology interrogations of dominant research practices
- Posthumanist, more-than-human, multispecies inquiry
- Ritual, ceremony, embodied-practice work with critical framing

### Not Recommended For

- Quantitative or mixed-methods (quantitative-dominant) work
- Conventional empirical-psychology submissions
- Manuscripts requiring extensive reference engagement (8,000-word ceiling is inclusive)
- Pure theoretical-psychology contributions (consider Theory & Psychology)
- Pure qualitative-psychology empirical work (consider Qualitative Research in Psychology)

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [Qualitative Inquiry](./qualitative-inquiry.md) | Larger word allowance; broader scope across qualitative methodology; same SAGE qualitative family |
| 2 | [Qualitative Research in Psychology](./qualitative-research-in-psychology.md) | If the work has psychology grounding; more accommodating word limits; T&F-published |
| 3 | [Theory & Psychology](../psychology/theory-and-psychology.md) | If the contribution is theoretical and Foucauldian / discursive rather than performative |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a more conventional qualitative-research outlet for being "too political" or "too experimental," CSCM is often a better fit because: it explicitly welcomes critical and decolonial framings rejected as activist by mainstream journals; performative and poetic forms are signature article types; and the editorial culture treats methodological disruption as a contribution rather than a flaw.
