### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** The aims frame submissions as contributing to "the ethical, social and political questions" of ICT use and development; no further required framing is stated (aims and scope, archive 2026-07-26: https://web.archive.org/web/20260726233259/https://link.springer.com/journal/10676/aims-and-scope).
- **Article-type framing:** Original Research Papers present "results of novel academic research"; Review Papers count as original research when they "significantly contribute to progress in a field of study", for example by "a concise overview and a new interpretation of the state of the art"; book reviews are invited (guidelines, archive 2026-07-02: https://web.archive.org/web/20260702043736/https://link.springer.com/journal/10676/submission-guidelines).
- **Declarations:** a "Statements and Declarations" section is required; submissions without relevant declarations "will be returned as incomplete"; author contribution and competing interests are entered in the submission interface (same page).
- **Topics of interest named:** technology assessment, cultural studies, public policy analysis and public administration, cognitive science, social and anthropological studies of technology, mass communication and legal studies, plus history of ideas on ICT (aims page, archive 2026-07-26); calls for papers of archive 2025-12-31 covered decentralised technologies and AI judgment and rules (journal page, https://web.archive.org/web/20251231202232/https://link.springer.com/journal/10676).
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: the guideline pages state no consequence for off-scope work; no desk-rejection rate is published.

### Voice & Style

- Guidelines (archive 2026-07-02): author-year citations; APA 7 author-count rules in the reference list; abstract of 150 to 250 words; 4 to 6 keywords; length of about 5,000 to 8,000 words for Original Research Papers. https://web.archive.org/web/20260702043736/https://link.springer.com/journal/10676/submission-guidelines
- Abstract length: measured median of 186 words over 227 Crossref abstracts, range 41 to 340 (the deposited text includes the leading word "Abstract"); the guideline range is 150 to 250.
- First person: 163 of 227 deposited abstracts (71.8%) contain I, my, we or our (Crossref, 2021-2026; case-sensitive pattern, so an approximation); none begins with a first-person pronoun. Of the 12 most recent abstracts retrieved (published 2026-08-13 to 2026-11-09, for example 10.1007/s10676-026-09929-7, 10.1007/s10676-026-09926-w, 10.1007/s10676-026-09919-9), 8 contain first person and 4 do not (10.1007/s10676-026-09932-y, 10.1007/s10676-026-09928-8, 10.1007/s10676-026-09924-y, 10.1007/s10676-026-09923-z).
- Double-blind: the manuscript and files must be free of identifying information, and authors should avoid citing their own work in a way that reveals their identity; where reviewers are given access to data, the guidelines strongly suggest anonymising authorship in the data (guidelines, archive 2026-07-02).

## Strategic Notes

> Each statement below follows from a fact recorded above; the source row is named in brackets.

### Hard Blockers

- Double-blind anonymisation of manuscript and files, with author details entered in the system interface (Peer Review > Type).
- A "Statements and Declarations" section is required; submissions without it are returned as incomplete (Format > Figure/Table limits).
- A data availability statement is mandatory for original research (Practical Concerns > Data transparency requirement).
- A maximum of 8,000 words of content, with tables and quotes counted (Format > Word limit).
- Editable source files (.docx or LaTeX) are required at each submission and revision (Format > Supplementary material).

### Soft Tax

- Time: no publisher-date acceptance figure exists; the journal's own first-decision median is 20 days, the APA Journal Survey sample (n=13, 2009 to 2023) averages 5.1 months to response and 4 months to publication, and Crossref shows 21 days from acceptance to first online (Review Cycle Time).
- Documentation of any LLM use other than copy editing in the Methods (AI Policy).
- Book reviews: the live guidelines (2026-11-23) say the journal does not accept requests to review individual books and publishes invited "author meets critics" reviews; the archive of 2026-07-02 said they should not be submitted without invitation (Format > Article types accepted).
- Open access, if wanted, depends on an APC not confirmed here (Open Access).

### Best Suited For

- Philosophical or conceptual analysis of ICT, AI and data ethics, which the aims name and which 45.4% of abstract-bearing records signal by wording (Subject Density > Orientation).
- Work that brings in legal, policy, media or social-science perspectives, which the aims list as in scope (Subject Density > Cross-disciplinary openness).
- Responsibility, privacy, fairness and health-related ethics, the keyword families with the highest shares (Sensitive Topics).
- Authors without an institutional affiliation: the guidelines describe how unaffiliated authors are recorded (Practical Concerns).

### Not Recommended For

- Manuscripts over 8,000 words of content (Hard Blockers).
- Work with no ethical or moral-philosophical angle on technology, since the aims define the journal as a dialogue between moral philosophy and ICT (Subject Density > Current positioning).
- Authors who need a documented review-time estimate: the sources checked provide only one journal median and a small survey sample (Review Cycle Time).
