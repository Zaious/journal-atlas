### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** The journal's scope is STS in a multi-disciplinary sense: science, technology and medicine as social and material activities (description page, https://web.archive.org/web/20220805204140/https://journals.sagepub.com/description/SSS).
- **Article-type framing:** original research, substantial discussion papers, short research notes and review essays; research articles at most about 12,000 words (guidelines, https://web.archive.org/web/20250701084205/https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/SSS).
- **Cover letter:** the submission asks for a cover letter saying why the manuscript is suitable, to help the editor's preliminary evaluation (same page, archive 2025-07-01).
- **Initial screening:** manuscripts that do not follow the guidelines are returned for amendment before review, and out-of-scope or otherwise unsuitable manuscripts may be desk rejected (same page).
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: desk rejection without review is the stated consequence; one SciRev review reports a fast rejection that advised a journal with a different focus (https://scirev.org/journal/social-studies-of-science/, January 2026).

## Strategic Notes

> Each statement below follows from a fact recorded above; the source row is named in brackets.

### Hard Blockers

- Scope is STS: the description positions the journal as a central STS venue, and out-of-scope manuscripts may be desk rejected (Framing Requirements; Peer Review).
- Anonymisation is required: a separate title page and a fully anonymised manuscript (Format; Voice & Style).
- Ethics approval or an explicit statement that none was required, plus a data availability statement, must be on the title page (Practical Concerns).
- Research articles are generally limited to 12,000 words (Format > Word limit).

### Soft Tax

- A cover letter justifying fit with the journal is requested (Framing Requirements).
- Time: no cycle-time figure is published by the journal or measurable in Crossref (Review Cycle Time), so turnaround cannot be planned from evidence.
- The reference style is not determinable from the archived page (Format > Reference limit).
- Open access is a paid option at a Sage-set fee in the 3,000 to 4,000 USD range for most Sage titles; the journal-specific figure was not found (Open Access).

### Best Suited For

- Qualitative STS work such as ethnography, interviews, laboratory studies and archival or historical studies: 17.8%, 15.2%, 8.7% and 17.4% of abstract-bearing records mention them (Methodological Preferences).
- Conceptual or discussion-style contributions, given "substantial discussion papers" is a named article type and 38.3% of records carry conceptual or argument wording (Format; Methodological Preferences).
- Studies of medicine and biomedicine as social practice: 25.2% of records carry such wording (Epistemological & Political Leanings).
- Longer manuscripts: the limit for research articles is 12,000 words, well above the 8,000 stated by the sister STS journal in this atlas (Format; see the Science, Technology, & Human Values entry).

### Not Recommended For

- Survey, statistical, mixed-methods or experimental work as the main contribution: those words appear in 6, 11, 3 and 1 of 230 records (Methodological Preferences).
- Work needing a predictable deadline, since no review-time figure is published (Review Cycle Time).
- Anyone needing a no-cost open-access route without an institutional agreement (Open Access).
