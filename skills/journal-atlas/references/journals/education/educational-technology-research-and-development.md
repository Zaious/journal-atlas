### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Papers must fall within educational technology or instructional design: the aims text prioritises research on "applications of technology or instructional design in educational settings" in K-12, higher education and adult learning (https://link.springer.com/journal/11423/aims-and-scope, retrieved 2026-11-23). A manuscript synopsis is mandatory and must state the novelty or under-researched aspect, the importance for educational technologies, key findings and implications (guidelines page, https://link.springer.com/journal/11423/submission-guidelines).
- **What kind of engagement?** The Development section expects papers to "provide evidence linking outcomes" to instructional-design decisions where possible; the Research section features "well-documented articles on the practical aspects of research as well as applied theory" (https://link.springer.com/journal/11423/aims-and-scope).
- **Article types and sections**: Research, Development and Cultural and Regional Perspectives sections, each with its own editor, plus Featured papers (guidelines page). Cultural and Regional submissions must be "situated in cultural contexts" and critically examine prevailing issues and ideologies (https://link.springer.com/journal/11423/aims-and-scope).
- **Length**: papers over 8,000 words are unlikely to be published; 5,000 words is called particularly welcome; abstract 150 to 250 words (https://link.springer.com/journal/11423/submission-guidelines).
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: submissions without a synopsis will not be reviewed (guidelines page). No source states what happens to out-of-scope submissions, and no desk-rejection data exists beyond a median of 22 days to first decision (home page).

## Strategic Notes

> Each statement below follows from a fact recorded above and names it.

### Hard Blockers

- **Synopsis**: submissions without the required four-part synopsis will not be reviewed (Framing Requirements; Format).
- **Anonymisation**: the manuscript and all files must be anonymised, with a separate title page; the duty sits with the author (Peer Review; Practical Concerns).
- **Ethics statement**: human-participant studies need a named ethics committee's approval or a stated exemption; retrospective approval usually cannot be obtained and may stop the paper going to review (Practical Concerns).
- **Scope**: educational technology or instructional design in K-12, higher education or adult learning (Framing Requirements; aims text).
- **AI use**: AI cannot be an author and generated scientific claims, results or figures without verifiable sources are not permitted (AI Policy).
- **Length**: papers over 8,000 words are unlikely to be published unless of exceptional significance (Format).

### Soft Tax

- **Preparation**: a synopsis of about 400 words across four sections, up to three suggested reviewers with institution, country and degree, and 4 to 6 keywords are required at submission (Format; Practical Concerns).
- **Anonymity versus preprints**: the publisher encourages preprints while the journal is double-blind; a public preprint can reveal authorship (Preprint Policy; Practical Concerns).
- **Open access cost**: GBP 2590, USD 3590 or EUR 2890 for gold open access; green deposit of the accepted manuscript to a repository waits 12 months, while a personal website is allowed at once (Open Access; Preprint Policy).
- **Time**: the median to first decision is 22 days (Review Cycle Time); no figure for later stages could be read, and SciRev's single review reports a 10.0-week first round (n=1).

### Best Suited For

- Empirical studies of technology-supported learning with quantitative, qualitative or mixed designs: all three are named as the Research section's highest priority, with 75 and 62 of 226 abstract records using quantitative and qualitative terms (Methodological Preferences).
- Design and development work with evidence linking outcomes to design decisions: the Development section states this and 14 of 226 abstract records use design-based or formative-evaluation terms (Methodological Preferences).
- Work on AI, generative AI, virtual reality and gamification in learning: 55 of 226 abstract records mention AI, 56 of 685 titles mention virtual or augmented reality, and two open special-issue calls concern AI (Subject Density; Sensitive Topics).
- Teams of two to five authors: 549 of 685 records (Practical Concerns).
- Culture- or region-specific studies that critically examine local ideologies, through the dedicated section (Epistemological & Political Leanings).

### Not Recommended For

- Primarily philosophical or purely theoretical essays without an educational-technology application: the aims text frames the journal around research and development, and 1 of 226 abstract records uses autoethnography or narrative-inquiry terms while 0 use ethnography terms (Methodological Preferences). This shows rarity, not rejection.
- Sole-authored manuscripts that need a typical sole-author outlet: 60 of 685 records (8.8%) are single-author (Practical Concerns); the counts show rarity, not rejection.
- Authors who need public preprints in circulation during review (Practical Concerns).
- Authors who need an acceptance rate or total turnaround before choosing: neither was found (Metrics; Review Cycle Time).
