## Format

> Sources: archived PDF print of the Sage guidelines page, 2024-12-05 (https://web.archive.org/web/20250323181742id_/https://www.sagepub.com/docs/default-source/msg/submission-guidelines_-educational-researcher_-sage-journals.pdf?sfvrsn=82c21c6e_5) and the older page of 2022-10-02 (https://web.archive.org/web/20221002154843/https://journals.sagepub.com/author-instructions/edr), which agree on every limit below. Pages change; check the live guidelines before submitting.

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Article types accepted** | Research articles in three kinds (feature articles, reviews/essays, briefs) plus technical comments; commentaries as policy forum, letters, and books et al. (reviews of books and media, solicited). Contact the editors for a genre or length that does not fit. |
| **Word limit** | Feature articles: text totaling no more than 5,000 words, up to five figures or tables and up to 40 references (longer allowed at editors' discretion). Reviews/essays: no more than 4,000 words, up to 40 references. Briefs: no more than 1,000 words, two figures or tables, six references, title of eight words or fewer. Technical comments: no more than 1,000 words and two figures or tables. Policy forum essays: 1,000–2,000 words plus one or two figures. Letters: up to 300 words. Books et al.: up to 1,000 words. |
| **Word limit negotiability** | Feature articles "may be somewhat longer than 5,000 words at the discretion of the editors"; no other flexibility is stated. Methods detail "should usually be included in supplementary online material". |
| **Abstract limit** | 75–120 words for all manuscripts except technical comments and commentaries (no more than 50 words); the brief-article paragraph separately says 100 words or less. |
| **Reference limit** | 40 for feature articles and reviews/essays, six for briefs (see word limits); whether references count toward the word total is not stated. APA style. |
| **Supplementary material** | Allowed; online supplementary material is the stated place for methods detail. Supplementary files in Word, RTF or Excel (2024 print). |
| **Figure/Table limits** | Five for feature articles, two for briefs and technical comments. Tables go at the end after the references, editable rather than images; figure captions on a separate page. Submission file in Word, RTF, WordPerfect or LaTeX; PDFs only to accompany LaTeX. Authors should consult AERA's "Standards for Reporting on Empirical Social Science Research" (2006). |

### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Broad significance for the whole education-research community: ER publishes work "of general significance" and "broad importance" (journal description, archive 2023-12-10: https://web.archive.org/web/20231210001206/https://journals.sagepub.com/description/edr). Narrow or single-context work is not excluded by any stated rule.
- **Article-type framing:** feature articles present "important new research results of broad significance"; reviews/essays "describe new developments of broad significance and highlight unresolved questions and future directions"; briefs are short analyses on one topic or question using new data or existing databases (guidelines print, archive 2024-12-05: https://web.archive.org/web/20250323181742id_/https://www.sagepub.com/docs/default-source/msg/submission-guidelines_-educational-researcher_-sage-journals.pdf?sfvrsn=82c21c6e_5).
- **Reporting standards:** authors consult AERA's "Standards for Reporting on Empirical Social Science Research in AERA Publications" (same guidelines print).
- **Policy link:** a Policy Forum section is dedicated to the intersections of education research and policy, and policy wording appears in 91 of 380 records (23.9%) (guidelines print; Crossref count, retrieved 2026-11-23).
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: the print says manuscripts not conforming to the guidelines "may be returned"; no stated consequence for lack of broad significance beyond editors' judgment.

## Strategic Notes

> Each statement below follows from a fact recorded above; the source row is named in brackets.

### Hard Blockers

- Length caps by article type: 5,000 words for feature articles, 4,000 for reviews/essays, 1,000 for briefs and technical comments, with only a discretionary exception for longer feature articles (Format > Word limit, Word limit negotiability).
- The main file must be fully anonymised, with identifying self-references removed (Peer Review > Type; Voice & Style).
- A general-significance test applies to every submission (Framing Requirements), and AERA's published acceptance rate was 9.8% in 2025 and 8.3% in 2024 (Metrics > Acceptance Rate).

### Soft Tax

- Methods detail has to move into online supplementary material to fit the length caps (Format > Word limit negotiability).
- Disclosure of generative-AI use in the methods or acknowledgements, with model and purpose named (AI Policy > Summary).
- Unclear turnaround: no decision-time figure is published; the one SciRev entry is a 16-day immediate rejection, which is a single data point (Review Cycle Time).
- Preprint rules are unstated, so authors who posted a preprint must check with the editors before submitting (Preprint Policy).

### Best Suited For

- Education-policy, teacher and schooling studies with a broad audience: policy wording appears in 23.9% and teacher wording in 28.2% of records, and a Policy Forum section exists (Epistemological & Political Leanings; Framing Requirements).
- Race and equity scholarship, quantitative or conceptual: racial wording appears in 21.3% of records and equity in 12.9%, and an intersectional framing piece is among the five most-cited records of the window (Epistemological & Political Leanings).
- Short empirical briefs using existing datasets: briefs are a named type with a 1,000-word cap and the guidelines name national statistics databases as a source (Format > Word limit; Orientation).
- Survey-based, longitudinal or administrative-data analyses, each at 10.5% of records (Methodological Preferences).

### Not Recommended For

- Long manuscripts that cannot be cut to 5,000 words (Hard Blockers).
- Single-site or niche studies with no argument of broad significance (Framing Requirements).
- Autoethnographic, discourse-analytic or mixed-methods-led work as the main method: 0, 2 and 3 of 380 records respectively in this window (Methodological Preferences).
- Work whose authors need a published turnaround guarantee (Review Cycle Time).
