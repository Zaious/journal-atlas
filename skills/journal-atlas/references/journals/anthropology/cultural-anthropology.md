### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes: an article should be "more than a solid ethnographic case study", drawing on original field research or historical evidence considered anthropologically, to contribute to contemporary conversations and theoretical developments (https://journal.culanth.org/index.php/ca/submission-guidelines, retrieved 2026-11-23).
- **What kind of engagement?** Engage and cite beyond the author's core networks and take alternative analyses seriously; proportionate claims; speak "relevantly and reflexively" to research ethics, design and methodology; an informative title and an abstract written with minimal jargon for a broad anthropological audience (same page).

- **Special-issue framing seen**: no special issues; Colloquy collections are guest-edited around a shared theme or concept (submission-guidelines page).
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: manuscripts outside the 7,500 to 9,500 word band are returned; the initial editorial review within 2 to 3 weeks can decline an article before external review (https://journal.culanth.org/index.php/ca/submission-guidelines, retrieved 2026-11-23). ## Strategic Notes

> Each statement follows from a fact recorded above and names it. Where evidence is missing the field says so.

### Hard Blockers

- **Length band**: manuscripts under 7,500 or over 9,500 words are returned (Format).
- **Membership or fee**: authors must be SCA members, join the SCA if in the AAA, or pay a $25 fee, before review (Open Access).
- **Anonymisation**: identifying references must be removed (Peer Review).
- **AI**: AI use in methodology or analysis must be disclosed; AI-generated content is limited to AI-topic papers (AI Policy).
- **Exclusive consideration**: no simultaneous submission elsewhere (Practical Concerns).

### Soft Tax

- **Time**: an initial editorial review takes 2 to 3 weeks, and decisions after external review "generally" take up to three months; no measured data (Review Cycle Time).
- **Citation work**: the guidelines expect engagement with scholars from the research region and with diverse canons, including non-English work (Framing Requirements).
