### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes (soft)
- **If yes, what kind?** Theoretical or meta-theoretical contribution must be foregrounded. The journal's stated scope is "theoretical and meta-theoretical analysis in psychology" — pure empirical reports without conceptual contribution are misaligned.
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: Likely desk reject if the editor reads the manuscript as "empirical study with no theoretical claim"; major revision requested by reviewers if theoretical depth is judged insufficient.

### Reviewer Pool Characteristics

Reviewers are drawn primarily from the International Society for Theoretical Psychology (ISTP) network and adjacent critical/theoretical psychology communities. Expect literacy in critical realism (Bhaskar, Fletcher), discursive psychology (Edwards, Potter), phenomenology (Merleau-Ponty, Gallagher), and Foucauldian analysis. Reviewers tend to be tolerant of methodological pluralism but exacting on theoretical rigor — a common failure mode is "interesting data, but where is the theoretical contribution?"

## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Manuscripts framed as pure empirical reports with no theoretical contribution
- Submissions over 10,000 words (returned for trimming before review)
- AI-generated text or figures without explicit disclosure and editor permission (treated as potential scientific misconduct)
- Manuscripts with substantially overlapping preprint updates posted during the review window

### Soft Tax

Minimal for theoretically-oriented manuscripts. Empirical authors typically invest ~1–2 revision sessions to strengthen the theoretical / meta-theoretical framing (e.g., explicitly positioning findings against an existing theoretical tradition). Citation tax: expect to engage with critical-realist, discursive, or phenomenological literature unless your contribution explicitly contests these traditions.

### Best Suited For

- Conceptual / theoretical papers proposing new frameworks in psychology
- Critical analyses of mainstream psychological constructs (e.g., critiques of trait-based personality models, cognitive computationalism)
- Phenomenological, discursive, or critical-realist empirical work where the theoretical contribution is foregrounded
- Meta-theoretical commentary engaging current debates in philosophy of psychology
- Cross-disciplinary work bridging psychology with philosophy, sociology, linguistics, or cognitive science

### Not Recommended For

- Strictly quantitative experimental work without theoretical critique
- Effect-size meta-analyses or registered reports prioritizing methodological rigor over conceptual novelty
- Clinical-application papers framed primarily as treatment effectiveness
- Short empirical notes with no engagement with theoretical traditions

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [Review of General Psychology](./review-of-general-psychology.md) | Similar conceptual / integrative scope; APA-published with strong reputation; welcomes broad theoretical syntheses across psychology subfields |
| 2 | [New Ideas in Psychology](./new-ideas-in-psychology.md) | Explicitly welcomes "speculative, fundamental, theoretical, and integrative" papers; lower theoretical-purity threshold; comparable Elsevier reputation |
| 3 | [Phenomenology and the Cognitive Sciences](./phenomenology-and-the-cognitive-sciences.md) | Stronger fit if the theoretical contribution is phenomenological / 4E-cognition oriented; no hard word limit; accepts long reference lists |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a more selective generalist venue (e.g., *Psychological Review*, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*), Theory & Psychology is often a better fit because: it welcomes interpretive and critical methodologies, does not require quantitative-experimental backing, and explicitly values meta-theoretical work that mainstream journals treat as out of scope.
