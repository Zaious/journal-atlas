### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes (strong)
- **If yes, what kind?** Manuscripts must engage mainstream social-psychology / identity-process theory with **quantitative or experimental** evidence. Qualitative or autoethnographic work without significant quantitative scaffolding is misaligned with the journal's house style.
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: Desk reject highly likely for pure qualitative or first-person work without experimental/survey backing; reviewer pushback for any single-study or N<100 design.

### Reviewer Pool Characteristics

Reviewers are drawn primarily from the International Society for Self and Identity (ISSI) and mainstream social-psychology community. Expect deep literacy in social identity theory, identity-process models (Marcia, Berzonsky, Luyckx), terror management theory, attachment theory, and quantitative methods (ANOVA, mediation, SEM). Common reviewer demands: "Where is the replication study?", "Effect sizes?", "What is the experimental manipulation?". Qualitative methodology framings will likely meet skepticism about generalizability and representativeness.

## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Pure qualitative or autoethnographic submissions without quantitative backing
- Single-study designs without strong theoretical justification
- Manuscripts without engagement with mainstream social-psychology identity theories
- Failure to disclose AI use per T&F publisher policy

### Soft Tax

High for qualitative / first-person authors. Adapting a primarily qualitative manuscript for S&I typically requires: (1) adding a quantitative study (or substantial restructuring), (2) recasting first-person voice into third-person scholarly register, (3) engaging social-identity / identity-process theory canon. Effectively a different paper. For quantitative social-psychology authors, the soft tax is minimal — house style is the journal's center of gravity.

### Best Suited For

- Multi-study quantitative / experimental social-psychology papers on self-concept, self-esteem, identity processes
- Terror management theory, social identity theory, attachment-related identity work
- Meta-analyses of identity-process or self-esteem literature
- Replications and registered reports in social-psychology identity domain
- Mixed-methods work with dominant quantitative component

### Not Recommended For

- Pure autoethnography or first-person reflective work
- Single-case qualitative analyses
- Theoretical / conceptual papers without empirical test
- Cultural-psychology dialogical-self work (better suited to Culture & Psychology)
- Critical / discursive psychology papers (better suited to Theory & Psychology)

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [Culture & Psychology](./culture-and-psychology.md) | Identity scope overlap; welcomes dialogical-self and qualitative approaches that S&I rejects |
| 2 | [Review of General Psychology](./review-of-general-psychology.md) | If the work is more integrative-theoretical than empirical-multi-study; APA Division 1 |
| 3 | [Frontiers in Psychology](./frontiers-in-psychology.md) | Section-specific routing (Personality & Social Psychology section); broader methodological tolerance |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a top social-psychology venue (e.g., *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, *Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin*), Self and Identity is often a better fit because: it shares the same theoretical-empirical paradigm but with somewhat lower acceptance bar; specialization in self/identity reduces "fit" rejections; and ISSI affiliation provides built-in audience.
