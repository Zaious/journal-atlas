### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes
- **If yes, what kind?** Manuscripts must engage phenomenological method or cognitive-science theory at the intersection. Pure cognitive-experimental work without phenomenological framing is misaligned; pure philosophical-phenomenological work without cognitive-science engagement is also a mismatch.
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: Desk reject likely if neither phenomenological nor cognitive-science engagement is foregrounded. Reviewers expect "phenomenology AND cognitive science" rather than either alone.

### Reviewer Pool Characteristics

Reviewers are drawn primarily from the phenomenology and 4E-cognition communities. Expect deep literacy in Husserl, Merleau-Ponty, Heidegger, and contemporary phenomenologists (Gallagher, Zahavi, Thompson, De Jaegher). Cognitive-science reviewers from predictive-processing and enactive-cognition traditions also present. Common reviewer demand: "How does this engage the phenomenological tradition rigorously, not just rhetorically?" Reviewers tolerant of long manuscripts when length is justified by argumentative depth.

## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Manuscripts that engage only phenomenology OR only cognitive science (must bridge both)
- Pure quantitative cognitive-experimental reports without phenomenological framing
- AI listed as author (per journal's explicit authorship statement)
- Failure to disclose AI use in Materials/Methods or Acknowledgments

### Soft Tax

Low for well-framed phenomenology-cognitive-science manuscripts. Authors typically invest ~1–2 revision sessions to: (1) deepen engagement with continental phenomenology canon when cognitive-science backgrounds are stronger, or (2) clarify cognitive-science stakes when philosophical backgrounds dominate. No hard word-limit pressure — length must be justified, but long manuscripts (10K+) are normal. Preprint strategy recommended to offset the 12-month AAM embargo.

### Best Suited For

- Theoretical papers at the phenomenology-cognitive science intersection
- Phenomenological analyses of embodied experience (gaming, body practices, disability, neurodiversity)
- Self-state, dialogical self, predictive processing, and 4E cognition syntheses
- Empirical phenomenological work (IPA, descriptive phenomenology) with theoretical contribution
- Long-form manuscripts requiring extensive reference engagement

### Not Recommended For

- Pure cognitive-experimental papers without phenomenological grounding
- Pure philosophical-phenomenological papers without cognitive-science engagement
- Short empirical reports without conceptual contribution
- Authors needing immediate post-acceptance open visibility (12-month AAM embargo)
- Effect-size meta-analyses or registered reports

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [Theory & Psychology](./theory-and-psychology.md) | Similar theoretical-conceptual openness; smaller word ceiling but no AAM embargo; welcomes phenomenological / discursive traditions |
| 2 | [New Ideas in Psychology](./new-ideas-in-psychology.md) | Strong fit for 4E / embodied / self-state work; cognitive-psychology framing aligned; 7.5K word ceiling is the trade-off |
| 3 | [Review of General Psychology](./review-of-general-psychology.md) | Broader integrative scope; APA Division 1; word limit 5–15K; welcomes "historical, theoretical, or critical studies" |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a top philosophy-of-mind venue (e.g., *Mind*, *Synthese*, *Philosophical Studies*), PCS is often a better fit because: it welcomes phenomenologically-grounded work that bridges to cognitive science; it accepts long reference lists and integrative arguments; and it values phenomenological method rather than analytic-philosophy register exclusively.
