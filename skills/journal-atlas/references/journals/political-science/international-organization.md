### Framing Requirements

- **Mandatory framing?** Yes, as a scope rule: "A study that does not emphasize any international (or cross-border) phenomenon as a major cause or effect falls outside the journal's domain" (preparing-your-materials page, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/information/author-instructions/preparing-your-materials; retrieved 2026-11-23).
- **What kind of engagement?** The page prefers manuscripts that apply or generate theoretical ideas, provide broader empirical analyses or contribute to scholarly debates over those that only propose a solution to a current world problem. A topic not yet featured in the journal is welcome; the page says the topic can be "old" or "new".
- **Article type and length**: research articles 14,000 words, research notes 8,000, essays 10,000 (see Format). A research note that introduces a new dataset is expected to include theory-driven analysis of it (same page).
- **Special-issue framing seen**: special issues are to advance the development of international relations theory and carry an introductory or concluding essay of 4,000 to 8,000 words in the proposal (same page).
- **Consequences of ignoring it**: the page reports that about 40 percent of submissions are judged by the editors not ready or appropriate for external review (review-process page, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/information/peer-review-information/review-process). The reasons are not broken down.

## Strategic Notes

> Each statement below follows from a fact recorded above and names it. Where the evidence does not support a statement, the field says so.

### Hard Blockers

- **International scope**: a study without an international or cross-border phenomenon as a major cause or effect falls outside the journal's domain (Framing Requirements).
- **Length**: research article 14,000 words, research note 8,000, essay 10,000, counting notes, tables and figures but not the bibliography (Format).
- **Anonymisation**: double-anonymized; self-references in the third person and no identifying content in the abstract (Peer Review, Format).
- **Originality**: work with half or more of its ideas or evidence published or under review elsewhere is not reviewed (Practical Concerns).
- **Data and replication**: quantitative papers must supply data and command files at conditional acceptance and wait for staff verification of every result (Practical Concerns).

### Soft Tax

- **Selectivity**: the journal states about 5 percent of submissions are published and about 40 percent are stopped at the editorial screen (Metrics).
- **Pre-registration and instruments**: survey-based work is strongly asked to pre-register and to attach instruments (Methodological Preferences).
- **Cost**: hybrid journal; the open-access route costs GBP 2610 or USD 3655 unless an institutional agreement applies (Open Access).
- **Time**: SciRev n = 3 reports first rounds of 4.7 to 7.3 weeks, all ending in rejection; no figure for accepted papers (Review Cycle Time).
- **Closures**: submissions during the announced winter and spring breaks wait until after them (Practical Concerns).

### Best Suited For

- Theory-led or empirically new studies of cross-border politics, using quantitative, experimental, formal-model, historical or qualitative designs: each of Quant, Experiment, Conceptual and History exceeds 19% of abstract-records (Methodological Preferences).
- Work on security, international institutions and political economy, the three largest wording groups in the window: 49.3%, 33.6% and 24.3% of abstract-records (Orientation).
- Authors who want the transfer route after a reviewed rejection: bilateral transfer is listed with three journals (Peer Review).

### Not Recommended For

- Single-country studies with no argument about wider relevance (Framing Requirements).
- Purely critical or post-structural international theory: matching wording appears in 4 of 140 abstract-records (2.9%, CriticalTheory), and the corpus neighbour Review of International Studies shows a far higher share (see that entry).
- Authors who cannot share data and code for quantitative results at conditional acceptance (Practical Concerns).
