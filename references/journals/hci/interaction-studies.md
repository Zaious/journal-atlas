## Strategic Notes

### Hard Blockers

- Manuscripts outside HRI / linguistics / comparative-cognition scope
- Pure usability HCI without interaction-theory framing
- Pure cognitive psychology without interactional anchor
- Failure to disclose AI use per Benjamins policy

### Soft Tax

Low for niche-specialty researchers. Moderate for mainstream HCI authors — requires recasting through interaction-as-phenomenon framing. Benjamins is a smaller publisher; readership niche.

### Best Suited For

- Social robotics and HRI empirical / theoretical work
- Interactional linguistics and conversation analysis
- Comparative cognition and animal communication studies
- Language and cultural evolution research
- Action observation and synchronization studies
- Cross-species / cross-modal interaction theory

### Not Recommended For

- Mainstream HCI usability papers (consider TOCHI, HCI journal)
- Pure cognitive psychology
- Pure design provocations
- Authors needing top-tier broad-HCI reputation signaling

### Rejection Fallback Chain

| Rank | Journal | Why this fallback works |
|------|---------|------------------------|
| 1 | [AI & Society](./ai-and-society.md) | If the HRI / robot-ethics angle is foregrounded; broader audience |
| 2 | [Frontiers in Computer Science](./frontiers-in-computer-science.md) | OA alternative; HMI section accommodates HRI work |
| 3 | [International Journal of Human-Computer Studies](./international-journal-of-human-computer-studies.md) | If the work has cognitive-engineering angle within HRI |

**Reverse direction** *(optional)*: If your paper was rejected from a mainstream cognitive-science or HCI venue as "too specialized / too HRI-focused," Interaction Studies is often a better fit because: it specifically welcomes HRI, linguistics, and comparative-cognition crossover; small specialty community provides focused review; and its niche identity rewards depth over breadth.
